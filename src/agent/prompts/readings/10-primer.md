## Your task: a primer

A primer gives the learner the terms that the lesson will use, before the lesson introduces them, so that when the teacher uses a word, the learner already has a clear grasp of it.

- **Give definitions only.** Each entry is a real definition: it says what the term is, stated so that it can be accepted at face value. It is not a list of properties, a derivation, or a theorem. If a term can only be explained by deriving it, give the one-line definition without the derivation. The derivation belongs to the lesson.
- **Choose the terms from the plan and the goal.** Pick the terms that the plan's nodes and the goal depend on, and that the probe suggests the learner does not yet hold firmly. Skip what the probe shows they already hold. Typically six to fifteen terms.
- **Order the entries** so that a definition only uses words that are everyday language or already defined above it.
- Each definition is one to three sentences, with notation in LaTeX where the term has one. An optional short "in plain words" gloss or a tiny example is fine, but a full paragraph is too long.
- `body` is a short intro of two or three sentences explaining what these terms are for. The glossary goes in `terms`, never repeated in the body.

## Your task — a primer

A primer gives him the **terms** the lesson will use, before he meets them in the lesson, so that when the teacher uses a word he already has a clean hold on it.

- **Definitions only.** Each entry is a real definition: what the term *is*, stated so it can be accepted at face value — not a list of properties, not a derivation, not a theorem. If a term can only be explained by deriving it, give the one-line definition and stop; deriving it is the lesson's job.
- **Choose the terms from the plan and the goal.** The terms the plan's nodes and the goal will lean on, that the probe suggests he doesn't firmly hold. Skip what the probe shows he already holds. Typically 6–15 terms.
- **Order** so a definition only uses words that are everyday or already defined above it.
- Each definition is one to three sentences, with notation in LaTeX where the term has one. An optional short "in plain words" gloss or a tiny example is fine; a paragraph is not.
- `body` is a short intro (two or three sentences: what these terms are for); the glossary goes in `terms`, never repeated in the body.
